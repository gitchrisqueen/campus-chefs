Register, search, eat. Chip in for the groceries.

A student who likes to cook shares a meal, and the students who eat it help pay for the groceries.

1Register.

Make an account with your email, a password, your name and your campus. Choose “I want to eat” or “I also cook”. You must be 18 or older. This is a demo, so use made-up details.

Cooks set up a kitchen: a short bio and a menu of breakfast, lunch and dinner dishes. Every dish lists its ingredients and its allergens, and the cook confirms that the allergen list is complete.

2Search.

Search by a cook's first name, or by a dish or ingredient. Filter by campus, meal, date and dietary tag, or leave out dishes that contain an allergen. Allergen and dietary details are declared by the cook, so they are not a safety guarantee.

3Eat!

Request 1 to 4 seats at a meal and confirm that you have read its allergens. The cook confirms or declines. After the meal, you can leave a review in two parts: “The good” and “The bad”.

When you book, you choose how to chip in toward the groceries:

  • $4.00 grocery share per seat
  • or bring an item
  • Free

Example amounts. The cook sets the share, or waives it and the meal is free. The site suggests a share from the grocery cost: $20.00 of groceries for 4 seats is $20.00 / (4 + 1) = $4.00 a seat, because the cook eats too. Suggestions round down to the nearest $0.25.

What chipping in means

  • A per-seat share of the grocery cost, or one item from the cook's bring list.
  • The shares for a meal can never add up to more than the cook's grocery estimate.
  • You settle up with the cook in person. The cook marks your share as received.

What the site never does

  • It never takes payments, card details or payment handles.
  • It never lets a cook charge for profit.
  • It never shows your email or your pickup address to the public.

Questions

Is this a real service?

No. It is a portfolio demo with fictional students and campuses. No real meals are served. Read the safety notes.

Can a cook make money?

No. Shares cover the groceries only, and the database refuses any share that would add up to more.

I have a food allergy. Can I rely on the allergen list?

No. Cooks declare allergens themselves and nobody checks them. If you have a severe allergy, do not rely on this site.

What happens to my data?

The site keeps as little as it can, and you can delete your account. See the privacy page.

About this project

The idea started as a student project in 2008: students who cook, students who eat, and groceries shared between them. This version is a portfolio rebuild with fictional people and campuses.
